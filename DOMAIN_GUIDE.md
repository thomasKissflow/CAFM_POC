# The domain, in this region: what a UAE contractor is actually dealing with

Written to help you hold your own in a Dutco conversation. Sources are listed at the end; **verify anything you intend to put on a slide**, especially the legal points — this is orientation, not legal advice.

Labels used: **FACT** with a source · **PRACTICE** (common in the market, not written in law) · **ASSUMPTION** (our demo's choice).

---

## 1. The one idea everything hangs off

A contractor's risk does not end when the building is handed over. It changes shape:

| Period | What it covers | Who carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **Defects liability, usually 12 months from the Taking-Over Certificate** | Ordinary defects: the AC that stops cooling, the tile that lifts, the leak | The main contractor fixes at its own cost (FACT) |
| **Ten years from delivery** | Total or partial collapse, and defects that threaten the building's stability or safety | Contractor **and** engineer, jointly and severally (FACT) |
| **Whatever the subcontract says** | Getting that money back from whoever installed it | Purely contractual (FACT) |

The third line is the commercial heart of it. Under the new UAE Civil Code (Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025, in force **1 June 2026**), Articles 821–824 confirm the ten-year regime **and state expressly that it does not govern the main contractor's recourse against its subcontractors** (Art. 821(4)). So the statute hands the contractor the liability, and the subcontract is the only route to recover. Agreements that try to exclude or limit the ten-year liability are void, and claims must be brought within three years of the collapse or of discovering the defect (Art. 824).

**Why this sells the app:** the money is only recoverable if, months later, you can prove *what* failed, *when*, *who installed it*, and *that the cause wasn't wear or misuse*. That evidence is created — or lost — in the first hour of a resident's complaint.

## 2. The handover moment (where the CAFM story starts)

- The **Taking-Over Certificate (TOC)** is the pivot: it starts the defects clock and is the reference point for the ten-year exposure (FACT).
- What changes hands: as-built drawings, O&M manuals, warranties, testing & commissioning certificates, spares — and, in a good project, an **asset register**. In a bad one, a box of PDFs (PRACTICE).
- **Snagging** happens before and around the TOC; anything not closed becomes a defect claim afterwards (PRACTICE).
- The asset register is the thing that makes liability answerable later: without knowing *which* fan coil unit is in Apt 1402, *which* batch it came from, and *who* installed it, every claim is a negotiation.

**In the demo:** the handover screen, the asset register with installer and batch, and the DLP clock per building all exist for this reason.

## 3. Who is who in a Dubai tower

| Party | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Developer** | Sells the units, hands over, often appoints the first FM company |
| **Main contractor** (Dutco's seat) | Built it; carries the defects and ten-year exposure |
| **Subcontractors** | MEP, lifts, façade, fit-out. Where most defects actually originate |
| **Owners' committee** | Owners' voice. Under Dubai's Law No. 6 of 2019 it is **not** a legal entity with budget control; members are appointed through RERA (FACT) |
| **Management company** | RERA-licensed; runs the building, sets the service-charge budget |
| **FM operator** | Helpdesk, PPM, the actual technicians. Sometimes the management company, often a separate contract |

**Money flows through Mollak**, RERA's system: the management company files a budget, an independent auditor reviews it, DLD approves it, owners are invoiced quarterly and pay into an escrow account tied to that building; funds cannot be spent outside the approved budget (FACT).

**Why it matters in a demo:** when a defect is the contractor's, it must *not* be paid from service charges. Getting that classification right, early and consistently, is the difference between money recovered and money quietly absorbed.

## 4. Fire and life safety: the compliance that has teeth

- The **UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice** is the standard; **Dubai Civil Defence (DCD)** enforces it.
- **Hassantuk** connects a building's fire alarm panel directly to Civil Defence's command centre, monitored 24/7 (FACT). Reporting indicates it is required for all commercial premises in Dubai and is checked at trade-licence renewal (**verify the current scope before quoting it**).
- The DCD certificate is renewed periodically (typically annual). Inspectors want the **AMC with a DCD-approved contractor**, test records, and a live Hassantuk connection (PRACTICE, consistently reported).

**Why it matters:** certificates expire on a date, not on a feeling. An FM team that cannot produce evidence on the day fails the inspection even if the work was done. That's the point of the compliance wall and the audit pack in the demo.

## 5. Cooling: the region's defining maintenance load

- Most large Dubai towers buy **district cooling** (chilled water) rather than running their own chillers; Empower alone serves 1,400-plus buildings (FACT).
- District cooling in Dubai is regulated: permit holders answer to the RSB, tariffs and charges are regulated, buildings have a master meter, and providers must give the building manager hourly data — supply and return temperature, flow, load, consumption (FACT).
- Inside the flat, the **fan coil unit (FCU)** is what the resident experiences. It is also the single most common complaint, and it fails in ways that are usually the installer's fault (valve actuators, controls, condensate) or the occupant's (filters, misuse).

**Why it matters:** "AC not cooling" is where liability, comfort and reputation collide. Deciding correctly whether it is a defect (contractor pays), wear (owner pays) or misuse (tenant pays) — in seconds, at the moment of the call — is exactly what the demo shows.

**ASSUMPTION in our demo:** the summer rule that raises AC faults from P3 to P2 between June and September, and the priority matrix (P1 30 min / 4 h … P4 1 day / 10 days). These are our numbers, meant to be replaced by Dutco's contract terms.

## 6. How the work actually runs

- **PPM** (planned maintenance) on calendar or meter cycles per asset class. Task libraries in the SFG20 style are common in GCC FM (PRACTICE — SFG20 is UK-origin; its use here is widespread but contract-specific).
- **Permit to work** for hot work, height, electrical isolation and confined space; issued, controlled, closed (PRACTICE).
- **Subcontractor governance**: trade licence expiry, insurance, DCD approval for fire contractors, and back-to-back defect obligations.
- **Seasonality**: summer drives AC demand and, with it, the helpdesk. Ramadan and the summer shutdown shift working hours and access windows (PRACTICE).
- **Language**: residents are a mix; requests arrive in Arabic and English, often by WhatsApp or phone rather than any system (PRACTICE). This is why the bilingual voice agent lands so well here.

## 7. Five questions that will come up, and short answers

**"Is this just a helpdesk?"**
No. A helpdesk records a complaint. This decides liability at the moment of the complaint, routes it to whoever is liable, and keeps the evidence needed to recover the cost.

**"Our subcontractors will dispute everything."**
That's why the photos, the fault cause and the timestamps sit on the job itself. A back-charge leaves the system with its evidence attached.

**"We already have a CAFM."**
Most CAFM systems are built for an FM operator, whose question is "was the job done?". A contractor's question is "who pays for it, and can I prove that in a year?". That's a different data model, and it's the one here.

**"What about the ten-year liability?"**
Structural reports route differently — engineering review, not a technician — and are tagged so they can be found years later. Note the new Civil Code confirms recourse against subcontractors is contractual only: your subcontracts and warranties have to carry it.

**"Can it work in Arabic?"**
The whole app, including the voice agent, which answers in the language the caller speaks.

## 8. Vocabulary (worth using correctly)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TOC** | Taking-Over Certificate — handover; starts the defects clock |
| **DLP / DNP** | Defects liability period / defects notification period — usually 12 months |
| **Decennial** | The ten-year liability for collapse or stability-threatening defects |
| **Back-to-back** | A subcontract mirroring the main contract's obligations, so liability passes down |
| **Back-charge** | Charging the cost of a defect repair to the subcontractor responsible |
| **Snag** | A defect found at handover inspection |
| **AMC** | Annual maintenance contract (fire systems must have one with a DCD-approved contractor) |
| **Hassantuk** | Civil Defence's 24/7 alarm-monitoring link |
| **Mollak** | RERA's service-charge platform: budget approval, invoicing, escrow |
| **RERA / DLD** | The regulator and the land department in Dubai |
| **RSB** | Dubai's Regulatory and Supervisory Bureau — regulates district cooling |
| **FCU** | Fan coil unit — the in-flat cooling unit; the most common complaint |
| **PPM** | Planned preventive maintenance |
| **PTW** | Permit to work |
